## Write the meaning directly

Write as though a person simply stated what the input means. Name the actor
and action when they are known. Turn nominalizations into verbs: "performs
validation" becomes "validates". Prefer "so the reader can compare the results"
to "allowing the results to be compared" when the reader is the actor. Keep
the passive when the actor is unknown or the recipient is what matters.
Concrete technical terms and natural contrasts are useful when they explain
the subject; replacing them with vague words does not make prose plainer.

## Preserve the statement before shortening it

Before editing, identify each fact, condition, permission, uncertainty,
implication and reason. The revision must still communicate all of them.
A consequence for the reader can be the point of an explanation: preserving
the implementation detail while deleting what it lets the reader do loses
meaning. Keep supported reasons even when they sound like a conclusion.

Use this deletion test for a clause, sentence or paragraph: if deleting it
changes no fact, condition, permission, uncertainty or implication, prefer
deleting it. Otherwise retain its meaning, either there or in a clearer
statement elsewhere. Do not assume readers will reconstruct a useful
consequence from its premises. Concision is the result of removing redundant
work for the reader, not a word quota.

## Preserve certainty in both directions

Keep the source's degree of certainty, including its scope and conditions.
Weakening an established claim is as much a meaning change as strengthening
an uncertain one. "May" is not "will"; "can" is not "does"; "not tested" is
not "incorrect"; "unsupported here" is not "impossible". Keep negation,
quantifiers, permissions and obligations. State established guarantees directly.

A supplied premise, an explicit contract, or code that covers the stated
conditions can establish a claim. A suggestive name, one branch of a check,
or an absence of tests cannot establish the opposite case. Distinguish
rewriting from fact-checking: if the source appears wrong, flag the discrepancy
and the evidence. Do not silently weaken it into a tautology or strengthen it
into a guarantee. When correction is in scope, make the correction explicit
and state the concrete behavior that the evidence establishes.

## Revise the whole explanation

Read the complete block or document before editing its sentences. Identify
the reader's question and the job of each paragraph. Put the answer before
the taxonomy, mechanism or history needed to understand it. Combine paragraphs
that do the same job; move detail to the point where the reader needs it.
Delete a paragraph only after applying the same meaning-preservation test.
Keep examples, qualifications and distinct results that answer a real question.

Then revise sentences for direct voice. Read the whole result again for gaps,
repetition, abrupt transitions and orphan conclusions. A set of locally clear
sentences can still be a poorly organized explanation. Report semantic
corrections separately from wording and structural edits.
